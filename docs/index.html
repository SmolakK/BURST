BURST

Balanced Urban Recommenders for Sustainable Mobility Transitions

Google Maps, Uber and TripAdvisor quietly decide where millions of people go and how they get there. BURST asks what those algorithms do to a city and designs recommenders that keep people moving without concentrating everyone in the same streets.

  • 2026–2028
  • 24-month postdoctoral fellowship
  • Pisa, Italy
How it works
The problem

Spatial filter bubbles

Urban Recommenders (URs) are the AI systems embedded in navigation, ride-hailing and review platforms and are designed for individual convenience. Because they steer everyone toward the same narrow set of popular places and routes, they concentrate activity, amplify popularity bias and reinforce a monocentric urban structure.

What happens

  • Traffic concentration, pollution and noise increase in a handful of neighbourhoods
  • Over-tourism and resident backlash at popular venues
  • Drop in footfall and economic activity in overlooked districts
  • Peri-urban shortcuts turn quiet lanes and cycling routes into through-traffic corridors

Why it is unstudied

Research on URs has focused on aggregate effects, such total congestion, emissions, and venue popularity. The individual-level formation of spatial filter bubbles, and their role in reinforcing spatial inequality, has not been systematically explored. Routing and destination recommendation are usually studied apart; BURST treats them as one system.

Today’s notion of “individual convenience” is also narrow: the fastest or shortest path, ignoring safety, pleasantness and broader urban wellbeing.

Cities are already reacting. For example, Barcelona removed a bus route from tourist apps to reduce overcrowding and protect local access. It is now urgent to understand how recommenders shape mobility and exposure, and how to mitigate it.
Research questions

Three questions, three objectives

Each research question maps onto a specific objective and a project phase.

RQ1 → SO1

Detect

How can we detect and quantify the presence and intensity of spatial filter bubbles in mobility trajectories? We will develop methods to detect and measure the intensity of spatial filter bubbles.

RQ2 → SO2

Measure

What are the long-term effects on congestion, socio-spatial segregation, environmental sustainability and the travel experience itself, including safety and perceived convenience? We will measure the impact of spatial filter bubbles in different settings.

RQ3 → SO3

Mitigate

Can we design recommenders that mitigate filter-bubble effects while aligning individual preferences with accessibility, equity and sustainability? This project will deliver a new class of Balanced Urban Recommenders (BURs).

Approach

State-of-the-art methodology

A modular, simulation-based methodology. High-resolution mobility data and open road and traffic datasets coupled with traffic microsimulations, UR simulations replicating commercial routing and destination strategies.

Phase 1

Detection & quantification

  • Collate high-resolution mobility, road and traffic datasets
  • Develop metrics for concentration, diversity and exposure
  • Empirically analyse individual and collective mobility patterns
Phase 2

Digital twins & simulation

  • Build data-driven mobility models for four case studies
  • Couple them with traffic microsimulations
  • Run A/B tests, scenario analysis and quasi-experiments
Phase 3

Balanced Urban Recommenders

  • Design BUR prototypes combining individual and collective goals
  • Benchmark against conventional popularity-driven URs
  • Evaluate on spatial equity, congestion, emissions and exposure

Expected results

What BURST will deliver

Everything is released openly, following FAIR and open-science principles: code and framework on GitHub, case-study analyses and data on Zenodo with permanent identifiers, papers in open access.

  1. 01Four empirical case studies — two urban, two peri-urban — each evaluated with at least ten indicators covering equity, mobility, congestion, exposure and emissions at individual and systemic level.
  2. 02Two BUR prototypes implementing alternative recommendation logics — diversity promotion, environmental sustainability, spatial equity — benchmarked against standard URs.
  3. 03An open-source toolkit bundling the metrics, the simulation framework and the BUR prototypes, with documentation and example case studies.
  4. 04Three peer-reviewed articles on filter-bubble detection, long-term UR impacts, and BUR design and evaluation.
  5. 05A policy brief translating the findings into actionable guidance for urban authorities on UR impacts and justice-aware recommender design.
